## Concept in one paragraph
A brand film for an EV-charging software company, cut to a voice-over. The narration is split into phrases of 2-5 words and each phrase is set on screen as text in one of three flat brand colours, usually with one small trick that acts out the key word (letters scattered for "complicated", flipped glyphs for "headache", an arched word for "freeing"). Stock footage only ever appears inside rounded cards, split panels or small tiles, never full-bleed for long. The single idea: the script itself is the picture, and the background colour flips on almost every phrase to mark the beat.

## Script-to-picture rules
- Problem statement: the phrase as text, plus stock footage in cards or behind blur; the key adjective is acted out by the letters (complicated, intimidating, headache).
- Product name: logo morph on a clean field. First mention: word at 12.23, mark appears 12.67, wordmark complete about 13.25 (lags 0.4-1.0 s). Last mention: word 69.63, wordmark complete at 69.5.
- Definition ("It's a ... software"): one line built word by word, then the product UI over footage.
- Feature list: a short text list with one active row, plus a changing footage card.
- Number / scale ("thousands of companies", "to all"): many images at once (tile grid) or fast swapping faces. No numerals are animated.
- Process / cause-effect: arrows between words ("feel -> confident", arrow carrying "managing").
- Call to action: none; the clip ends on the logo.
- Lead/lag (word start vs first visible): "electric" word 0.73 / seen 0.53; "car" 1.15 / 0.83; "complicated" 8.21 / assembled 8.25; "intimidating" 9.45 / flash 9.03, readable 9.57; "tools" 46.02 / 45.5; "services" 46.78 / 46.25; "buy" 49.72 / 49.25; "use" 50.48 / 50.0; "monetise" 51.18 / 50.75; "product" 35.11 / 34.75; "partner" 35.85 / 35.5. Text usually appears 0.2-0.5 s before its word is spoken; hero-word effects finish on or just after the word.

## Patterns to reuse in a talking-head explainer
1. **Phrase card with colour flip** -> any short claim the speaker makes. Full-frame flat colour, the phrase centred at about 6% frame height, built word by word at 0.3-0.4 s per word, held 0.5-1 s; next phrase on a different one of three brand colours.
2. **Act out the adjective** -> the speaker names a feeling or quality (complicated, overwhelming, easy). Scatter the letters and assemble them (0.5 s), or scramble then resolve (about 0.75 s), or show the word cropped too big for 15 frames then snap it to size.
3. **Text + footage card + pill label** -> naming two or three things or risks ("product or partner"). Rounded card, then a filled pill label popping in 0.3 s before the word; second card and pill overlap the first.
4. **Active-row list** -> listing 3 steps or options ("buy, use, monetise"). All rows visible, inactive dimmed, a dot and arrow on the active row, rows shift one step per spoken item; swap a picture beside it on each step.
5. **Zoom into the logo letter** -> "At <company>, we ..." Show the name small, zoom into one letter in about 18 frames until it fills the frame, then place feature pills inside that shape.
6. **Small-to-huge payoff** -> the last word of a promise ("is worthwhile"). Set the lead-in as very small text, grow one small element for 6 frames, hard cut to the payoff word at about 40% frame height, fill its letters one frame each.
7. **Image in place of a noun** -> "the way we [thing] ..." Put a small photo tile inside the sentence where the noun would be; for "everyone/all", swap the photo every 6 frames.
